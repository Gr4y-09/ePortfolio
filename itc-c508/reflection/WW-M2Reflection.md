This laboratory activity reflection looks back at our previous project proposal, which focused on developing a RAG chatbot for disaster-resilient infrastructure and flood preparedness using DOST-PAGASA and OCD/NDRRMC guidelines.

When we presented that paper, and as you know it didn't pass, the main feedback we got was that no normal citizen would actually open their phone to consult a chatbot about that matter. It was a tough critique, but it made total sense when people are facing an impending flood or trying to figure out infrastructure protection, messaging a chatbot is rarely the first thing on their mind.

Looking at what could be better, our previous topic suffered from a major disconnect between practical user behavior and academic project design. While building a RAG pipeline over government disaster documents sounded technically cool on paper, it lacked real-world urgency and personal applicability for a everyday user. Citizens in flood-prone areas rely on sirens, official weather alerts, local barangay announcements, or physical evacuation plans rather than interactive AI Q&A sessions.

Because of that failure, we realized we needed a topic where individuals genuinely look for guidance, need privacy, and require structured, step-by-step information during critical moments. This led us to our new title and project direction: focusing on violence against women (VAW) laws and what to do according to local municipality handbooks.

Shifting the topic to VAW guidelines and municipal handbooks makes a lot more sense because victims or concerned individuals often seek discrete, immediate, and accurate legal steps regarding protection orders, hotlines, and local social welfare resources. A localized RAG system grounded in municipal handbooks and legal statutes addresses a genuine information gap where people might feel too hesitant to immediately approach authorities or sift through dense legal jargon.

Overall, this whole experience taught me that choosing a research topic isn't just about showing off technical RAG pipelines or matching a Sustainable Development Goal (like SDG 9). It has to solve a real human problem where the user interface actually matches how people behave in the real world.
